# Meeting: AI Capability Development Strategy – India Engineering Team

Attendees: Head of Domain, India Engineering Manager, Yourself\
Duration: 45–60 minutes\
Purpose: Align on a strategy for developing AI engineering capabilities within the existing India development team.

## 1. Opening – Why are we having this discussion? (5 minutes)

I wanted us to have a discussion about the longer-term direction of our India engineering team, particularly how we prepare the team for AI-driven software development.

Today, we have an established team with capabilities across Java backend development, databases, Angular frontend development, and QA automation using Selenium and Playwright.

These skills remain relevant, but the nature of software development is evolving with AI.

I believe we have an opportunity to develop the team beyond traditional application development, positioning them to contribute to AI-enabled applications and potentially agent-based solutions.

Rather than immediately identifying training courses or asking the team to learn particular frameworks, I think we should first align on the strategy.

Specifically:

- What AI engineering capabilities do we want within the team?
- How ambitious should we be in terms of AI development?
- Should we evolve the entire team or develop a smaller group of AI specialists?
- How do we balance this transformation with existing delivery commitments?
- What support and investment would be needed?

The outcome I'd like from today's discussion is an agreed direction that we can translate into a practical capability development plan.

## 2. Strategic discussion topics

### Topic A – What is our ambition for the team? (10 minutes)

The first decision is what we want the India team to become.

There are three potential levels of ambition.

Option 1 – AI-augmented engineering team

Continue with traditional application development, but use AI tools to improve productivity, development speed, testing, and quality.

Lowest transformation effort

Option 2 – AI-enabled application development team

Develop the capability to build applications that incorporate LLMs, AI APIs, RAG, intelligent workflows, and AI features alongside existing enterprise technologies.

Moderate transformation effort

Option 3 – Advanced AI and agentic engineering team

Establish the capability to design and implement more sophisticated AI solutions, including agents, orchestration frameworks, tool integrations, and autonomous workflows with appropriate controls.

Highest transformation effort

Questions for the Head of Domain:

- Where do we see AI fitting into our domain's technology strategy over the next 12–24 months?
- Are we looking primarily for productivity improvement or the ability to deliver new AI-powered software?
- Do we foresee our team owning AI application development, or mostly integrating AI services developed elsewhere?
- Should agentic AI be a strategic capability we actively develop?

My proposed position: Target Option 2 as the core capability, while selectively building Option 3 expertise. Option 1 should be a baseline expectation across the team.

### Topic B – Build the capability broadly or create specialists? (10 minutes)

We should agree on the operating model before defining individual learning plans.

| Approach                              | Advantages                                        | Challenges                                  |
| ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| Upskill everyone equally              | Broad capability, flexibility                     | Slower progress, diluted effort             |
| Establish a small AI specialist group | Faster depth and experimentation                  | Knowledge concentration and dependency      |
| Hybrid model                          | Shared fundamentals with a few deeper specialists | Requires coordination and knowledge sharing |

My proposed position: A hybrid model.

Develop AI literacy and practical integration skills across the team, while identifying a small number of engineers who can develop deeper expertise in AI architecture, frameworks, RAG, and agent-based development.

The specialists would help establish patterns, evaluate technologies, and share knowledge with the wider team.

Questions to discuss:

- Do we want every Java developer capable of building AI-integrated services?
- Should we appoint one or two AI technical champions?
- Should frontend and QA have their own AI-specific expertise?
- Is the India Engineering Manager positioned to drive this transformation alongside regular delivery responsibilities?

### Topic C – Which capabilities should we prioritize? (10 minutes)

Rather than selecting frameworks immediately, agree on the capability areas that are strategically relevant.

| Capability area                             | Strategic importance                                  |
| ------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| AI-assisted engineering productivity        | Improve existing delivery effectiveness               |
| LLM integration and AI-powered applications | Enable new features in enterprise applications        |
| RAG and enterprise data integration         | Connect AI with domain knowledge and existing systems |
| AI frameworks and architecture              | Build maintainable, scalable AI solutions             |
| Agentic AI and workflow automation          | Prepare for emerging software patterns                |
| AI testing, evaluation, and security        | Ensure reliable enterprise AI delivery                |

Questions:

- Which of these areas are most relevant to our existing and future projects?
- Should we focus on Java-native AI frameworks such as Spring AI and LangChain4j, or build some Python capabilities?
- Should we build internal AI expertise or rely on centralized AI/platform teams where available?
- How important is understanding AI agents versus conventional AI integration?

My proposed position: Prioritize enterprise AI integration, RAG, AI architecture, and testing. Introduce agentic AI as a deliberate next step rather than the sole focus.

### Topic D – How do we invest without affecting delivery? (10 minutes)

This is likely to be one of the most important leadership decisions.

Upskilling cannot depend entirely on developers finding spare time.

We need to consider how to build capability without compromising existing commitments.

Possible investment models

Model 1 – Learning within existing capacity

Small, regular time allocation for AI learning.

Minimal short-term disruption, potentially slower progress

Model 2 – Protected innovation capacity

Allocate a defined portion of engineering capacity to structured learning and experiments.

More consistent capability development; requires delivery trade-offs

Model 3 – Dedicated AI capability initiative

Assign selected engineers to a focused AI initiative for a defined period.

Fastest potential progress, but greater effect on regular delivery

Questions for leadership:

- How much engineering capacity are we comfortable investing?
- Can we allocate protected learning time?
- Do we have a budget for training, LLM APIs, cloud services, and experimentation?
- Can we connect the learning activity with actual domain problems?
- How should we balance short-term productivity with longer-term capability building?

My proposed position: Start with protected capacity and a small group of technical champions, rather than immediately creating a separate AI team.

### Topic E – How do we know the strategy is working? (5 minutes)

We should agree on what success looks like at leadership level, without committing to individual technical deliverables today.

Possible success indicators after 6–12 months:

- The team can independently assess opportunities for AI integration.
- Developers can design and implement AI-enabled features within our existing technology stack.
- The team has practical knowledge of relevant AI frameworks.
- QA has the skills to evaluate and test probabilistic AI behaviour.
- The team can prototype controlled agent-based workflows.
- AI skills are distributed sufficiently to avoid reliance on one or two individuals.
- We can demonstrate business relevance, not just technical experimentation.

Important distinction: We are measuring capability maturity and business value, not the number of AI certifications completed.

## 4. Closing statement

My view is that we should treat this as a strategic evolution of our engineering capability, rather than a standalone training exercise.

We already have a strong foundation in enterprise application development. The opportunity is to build on that foundation and prepare the team to deliver AI-powered solutions, including agent-based systems where they add value.

I would suggest we adopt a phased approach: establish broad AI knowledge, develop deeper expertise in selected areas, and validate that capability through practical application.

If we can align today on the ambition, priority capabilities, operating model, and level of investment, we can then ask the India Engineering Manager to develop a more detailed roadmap for our review.

That would give us a clear direction without committing prematurely to specific tools, technologies, or delivery dates.

## One additional strategic question I would raise

"Are we upskilling the India team to support AI initiatives defined elsewhere, or do we want the team to become capable of identifying, proposing, and delivering AI solutions independently?"

This distinction matters significantly.

If the team is primarily an execution capability, the emphasis will be on understanding AI frameworks and implementing defined solutions.

If we want it to become a more strategic engineering capability, we also need to develop AI solution design, architectural decision-making, identifying business use cases, technical evaluation, and ownership of AI delivery.

My recommendation would be to aim for the second model over time.

The ideal outcome of today's meeting is agreement on five things: ambition, capability priorities, team model, investment, and ownership. The specific 90-day learning roadmap should be a follow-up, not the main discussion today.
